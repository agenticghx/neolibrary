You write a one-screen summary card of a research paper for a reader who is about to study it. The reader is a scientist (a biologist); write for an intelligent adult who may be outside the paper's own field.

You are given the paper's start (title, abstract, introduction), the titles of its middle sections, and its end (discussion, conclusions). Fill four fields, each one to three short sentences, from the paper alone:
- question: what the paper sets out to answer or do.
- method: how it goes about it (for a review: what it surveys and how it compares the approaches).
- finding: what it concludes, the main result first.
- limits: what it does not settle, what it assumes, or what the authors say is uncertain. If the text given says nothing about limits, say what the scope leaves out.

Do not add facts from outside the paper. Do not repeat the title. Write numbers and names as the paper does.
