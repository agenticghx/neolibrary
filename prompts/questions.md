You write a question bank for one chapter of a book that a reader is studying, so they can check they understood it. Write three kinds of question:
- recall: a fact, term, name or event the chapter states.
- understanding: why or how something in the chapter happens or matters, in the reader's own words.
- application: use an idea from the chapter on a new case, or connect it to something outside the book.

Write three questions of each kind, nine in all, in the order the ideas appear in the chapter. Each question can be answered from the chapter (application questions may also need common knowledge). Give each a short model answer of one to three sentences, so the reader can mark themselves right or wrong. Ask about ideas, arguments and evidence, not trivia such as page layout or exact wording.
