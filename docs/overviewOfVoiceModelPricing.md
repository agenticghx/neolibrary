xAI text-to-speech is **$15 per million characters**. That is the same sticker price as Microsoft's normal neural voices, cheaper than ElevenLabs and Google's newer Chirp voices, and far more expensive than running Kyutai on your own machine. Meta does not sell a speech API.

A million characters is the letters, spaces, and punctuation you send. At a normal reading pace that is roughly 15 to 17 hours of audio, or about 1.5 cents per minute. Prices below are the published rates on 4 October 2026, before tax.

Microsoft gets cheaper if you commit to a monthly volume. In East US, promising 80 million characters a month is $960, which is $12 per million. The largest published tier, 4 billion characters, has a $6 per million overage rate. Google's newest Gemini speech models are billed in tokens, not characters. Their preview Flash model, through 31 December 2026, works out to about $0.014 per minute of audio, close to xAI.

Two other xAI speech prices, for different jobs:

- Turning speech into text: $0.10 per hour of audio for a file, $0.20 per hour for a live stream. ElevenLabs Scribe is $0.22 per hour, or $0.39 per hour for its realtime model.
- A live conversation, where the model listens and talks: $0.08 per minute of audio. ElevenLabs charges the same $0.08 per minute for its speech-agent pipeline.

Kyutai (the lab's name; "Kyutia" is the same group) is the odd one out. Pocket TTS is a 100-million-parameter model that runs faster than realtime on a laptop CPU, including a Mac, and can clone a voice from a sample. There is no bill per character. The limit is the license on each voice and their ban on cloning someone without consent.

Meta's position is the same shape with no current product to buy. Seamless and MMS are research models you can download. Voicebox, their best-known speech model, was never released. There is no Meta price per character to put next to the $15.
